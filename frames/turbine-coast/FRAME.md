# Turbine Coast

Turbine Coast — a pale-daylight offshore array where the only warm colour is the one that means live.

## Palette

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|

| white (panel) | #fafcfc |

| signal (live) | #e8641f |

## Type

Michroma (display) — wide, squared, engineered — set 86–132px with 0.03em extra tracking. Every beat forces uppercase. Saira (sans) does labels, list rows, chip text and the in-world wind readout, so the type in the backdrop and the type in the layout are the same family.

## World

A working array, drawn behind hook / feature / stats / app. Three tapered white towers at scale 1.0 / 0.7 / 0.5, each with a three-blade rotor turning at a different rate (nearer turbines spin faster) and a signal-orange nacelle hub. A seagreen horizon rule at H−340 with a translucent sea filling below it. Three white swell lines scroll left at staggered speeds and fading opacity. A two-arc gull drifts on a slow sine near the top-left. Top-right, a Saira readout prints "WIND 12–17 KN" and ticks with the clock.

## Motion

Cameras: pushL, zoomOut, pushU, pushR, zoomIn, drop — stepped by 5 from index 3. Magnitudes: rot 0.4, slide 0.3, driftX 7. Both titles and items enter on `rise` — no bounce, no overshoot anywhere in the pack. Montage tilts are all literally 0°, chips are square, cards are 16px and stats carry a rule. Ambient clock 1.7.

## Beats

- **Toggle (dial)** — a 2×2 grid of rotary dials whose needles sweep from −120° to their set point and gain a ring when they land.
- **Code (terminal)** — a near-black `$` panel typing line by line behind a blinking block cursor, closing on a green ✔.
- **Notify (side)** — alert cards slide in from the right, each with a draining accent bar beneath it.
- **Morph (flap)** — a split-flap board: characters flip on individual tiles to reveal the next value.
- Ring falls through to the default radial dial for array output.

## Best for

Renewables and utilities, marine and industrial operations, infrastructure and logistics, hardware telemetry, control-room and monitoring products — anything that has to look measured rather than excited.
