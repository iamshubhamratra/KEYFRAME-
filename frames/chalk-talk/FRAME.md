# Chalk Talk

A lesson being drawn in front of you: dark green board, wooden ledge, handwriting, and diagrams that stroke themselves on every cut.

Board and second chalk are tweakable (board → slate blue #26333d or brown #3a3330; yellow → mint #a8d8a0 or sky #a0c8e8).

## Type

Gochi Hand — a marker face — writes every headline in sentence case, 94px on montage to 146px on the statement, line-height 1.12. Neucha, also handwritten, runs kickers, chips, tile labels and stat captions. The kicker is an outline tag in pink. Nothing is uppercased; nothing is straight.

## World

A wooden ledge fills the bottom 110px in #8a6a4a, carrying a 130×26 chalk stick at 85% chalk and a 60×22 pink eraser. Two chalk drawings **stroke themselves against scene progress, not the clock**: a yellow lens/eye shape at the top right draws between p 0.05 and 0.60, a pink triangle at the lower left between p 0.30 and 0.80 — so each cut restarts the drawing, as if someone is at the board now. A 5%-opacity chalk ellipse rotated −14° drifts `sin(0.4t)*100` across the board: the smear where it was wiped. Five 3px chalk dots near the top jitter on `sin(t+i)*8` — dust. Ambient clock 1.7; world shows on hook, feature, stats and app beats.

## Motion

Cams pushR, drop, pushL, zoomOut, hopU, spin (mul 3 — the slowest index churn here, offset 2) with `rot 1.2, driftX 7`: the strongest rotation of the group. Everything sits a degree or two off-square, hand-pinned. Titles **stamp**; items **pop**.

## Beats

- **Typing (hand)** — a line appearing in handwriting, stroke by stroke.
- **DragDrop (drag)** — labels dragged onto a diagram, or names onto a seating plan.
- **Toggle (check)** — a register or safety list ticking off row by row.
- **Morph (roll)** — one word rolling over into the next.
- Feature uses a **paper** card — a cream sheet pinned to the board — so photos and screenshots read as handouts; chips are outlined in yellow/pink/chalk; montage drops to ink with board-coloured tiles tilted −3° / 2.5° / 2° / −2.5°; the CTA is ink with a yellow pill button.

## Best for

Schools and universities, online courses and tutoring, explainers and how-it-works films, workshops and training, museums and science communication, non-profits and community programmes, kids' and family products.
