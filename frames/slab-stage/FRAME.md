# Slab Stage

**One take, not a stack of pages.** Where most templates cut between separate compositions,
this one is a single moving stage: the camera's pan, breath and drift run off the **film**
clock rather than the scene clock, so the move carries straight through every cut. Each cut
adds a short whip that resolves, the way a camera settles after an operator reframes.
Portrait-native **9:16**.

## The stage

- **Slabs** — flat 3D planes on a shared vanishing point (`perspective`), each rotated on Y
  and X, depth-scaled and depth-shadowed. Every slab **breathes on its own phase**, so
  nothing in frame is ever perfectly still.
- **Grounds alternate** light and dark scene to scene, both tinted by the brand, with soft
  accent pools bleeding in from opposite corners.
- A faint **modular grid** (5 × 10) sits under everything and moves with the camera.
- **Line-art props** — a clock, concentric rings, a radial burst — draw themselves on with a
  stroke-dash sweep and sit behind the content.
- A **progress bar** in the brand gradient runs the full width of the film.

## Type — karaoke

The pack's signature. Copy arrives **word by word**: the live word snaps large in the accent
while words already spoken hold back at low opacity and words still to come sit fainter
still. Each new word carries a short **motion-blur smear** on the attack that is gone by the
settle. Behind it, the live word is echoed as **huge outlined ghost letters**, tiled and
drifting slowly upward.

Word timing is derived from the scene's own duration, so the line always completes inside
its beat regardless of how long the script's copy runs.

## Transitions

A depth and dimension vocabulary — 3D slab flips, depth dives, stack shuffles, fold-aways,
whip pans — plus the signature **cut bar**: a brand-gradient plane that crosses the frame and
leaves. A sequencer deals them so no move repeats within three cuts.

## Assets

Screenshots ride the slabs themselves, sized from each asset's own ratio so nothing stretches
or crops, with an optional label bar naming the slot. Stock photography prints in black and
white; the user's own material — website captures, uploads and the logo — keeps full colour.

## Best for

Bold product launches, agencies, creative tools, and anything that should feel kinetic,
physical and staged rather than flat.
